Kick Craft

Draft 45

The LFO here is a GATE at 4 steps a beat, with the Euclidean pattern of the bank: TRIGS of the STEPS play. The lit cell is the step now.

Each change is saved as you make it, in a copy of the open preset. Load opens a copy: the saved preset changes only with Update Original. A preset keeps every knob of BODY, TEXTURE, CLICK, RUMBLE and LFO.

What it is

A kick drum on OP6. It has three layers, and each one has its own attack. The body and the click are two oscillators of the same kind, with the controls of the Shakmat Battering Ram: a pitch, a sweep of the pitch, a decay. The click is the short one. The body has a second output, the texture: the body with noise, through a low-pass gate and a reverb. The rumble comes up after each hit, as on the Ohmforce Bohm. Any LFO of the bank plays it.

This page plays the same algorithm in your browser, sample by sample. “Show” sets the length of the picture in milliseconds. The lines of the picture are parts of a bar at the TEMPO: they move with the tempo, and the wave does not. The K key hits the kick while a control of the instrument has the focus. It stays as a web app when the kick is in the FM-1.

How I would build it

One hit, from the trigger to the output. The amber name is the control for that step.

  1. 1. TriggerALGORITHM: the LFOThe chosen LFO goes up through 1 V. The two oscillators start again at the same place, so each hit is the same. The hit before fades in 2 ms. The rumble does not start again: its tail goes on under the next hit.
  2. 2. Pitch envelopeSWEEP, DEPTH, PITCH CURVEThe body’s pitch starts SWEEP octaves above the note and falls to it in the DEPTH time, with the shape of its PITCH CURVE. PITCH, DEPTH, SWEEP and PITCH CURVE have a value at each of 1 to 5 markers, and the body’s MOD LEVEL moves the body from the first marker to the last, as the click’s does.
  3. 3. Click oscillatorLEVEL, TUNE, SWEEP, ATTACK, SNAP, MODEThe same as the body, but short: a sine at TUNE, 40 Hz to 100 Hz, whose pitch starts SWEEP octaves above and falls with a PITCH CURVE of its own. Its level comes up in its ATTACK, 0 to 51 ms, then falls in SNAP, 5 ms to 50 ms. TUNE, SNAP, SWEEP, ATTACK, PITCH CURVE and NOISE have a value at each of 1 to 5 markers, and MOD LEVEL moves the click from the first marker to the last: between two markers the values are mixed.
  4. 4. Body oscillatorSELECT: the note, PHASEA sine at the note plus the pitch envelope. PHASE is where in its cycle it starts, 0 to 357 degrees: at 0 it starts soft, at 90 with a hard edge, at 180 it goes down first. In FM mode the click bends it; in RING mode the click multiplies it.
  5. 5. NoiseNOISE, TONEA burst of 15 ms at the hit, through a low-pass filter. It is part of the click, but the click’s ATTACK does not move it.
  6. 6. Body levelATTACK, ATK CURVE, DECAYThe DECAY starts at the hit and does not move. The ATTACK only cuts the start of the body, for 1 ms to 400 ms, so the click has the room; after that time the wave is the same as with no attack. ATK CURVE is the shape of the cut: the body stays low and comes up at the end (127), a straight line (42), or up at once (0).
  7. 7. DriveDRIVE, FOLD, CLIP or CRUSHAfter the envelope: the tail gets cleaner as it falls. In MIX mode the click and the noise go round it, so a loud body does not flatten them. In FM and RING the click is inside the body and takes the drive.
  8. 8. Low cutHPF30 Hz, 3rd order.
  9. 9. TextureLEVEL, ATTACK, DECAY, DCY CURVE, OPEN, NOISE, REVERB, SIZE, LOW CUT, HIGH CUTThe body’s second output. The body after its drive and its low cut, before its LEVEL, gets NOISE and goes into a low-pass gate: one envelope that is a low-pass filter and a level at the same time, as a vactrol gate. At the hit the gate opens to OPEN, 100 Hz to 12.8 kHz, after its ATTACK, 0 to 200 ms. Then it closes in DECAY, 20 ms to 2 s: the highs go first, then the level. DCY CURVE is the shape of the way down: fast at the start, then slowly (0), a straight line (42), or open for most of the time and down at the end (127). At each curve the gate is closed at the end of DECAY. REVERB adds a tail to what comes out of the gate, and SIZE is the time the tail takes to fall to nothing, 100 ms to 3.2 s. Two filters end the texture, after the reverb: LOW CUT takes out what is below it, 20 Hz to 2 kHz, and HIGH CUT takes out what is above it, 200 Hz to 16 kHz. Each is off at the end of its knob. The body itself, the raw output, does not change.
  10. 10. Four levels, then the mixLEVEL of each layerThe body, the texture, the click and the rumble each have a LEVEL. No level moves another layer: with the body at 0 you hear the texture, the click and the rumble alone. Then the sum, in the centre, before the compressor.
  11. 11. RumbleLEVEL, ATTACK, DELAY, REVERB, SIZE, LENGTH, DARK, GRIT, PHASE, LFOThe kick after its drive, before the body’s LEVEL, goes into four short echoes that feed themselves (a small reverb), then a low-pass at DARK and a drive of its own. Each hit pushes it down, and it comes up again in its ATTACK time, 5 ms to 1 s. PHASE makes it later by up to one cycle of the body’s note, so its lows add to the body or take from it. SIZE is the time the echoes take to fall to nothing, 150 ms to 2.4 s. It is not on the clock, so the tempo does not change the rumble’s tone. LENGTH is where the rumble closes after a hit, as a part of a bar at the tempo: 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 bar, 2 bars or 4 bars. It closes a gate and nothing else, so the rumble stops on the clock. A new hit starts the count again. DELAY puts repeats of the kick before the echoes: DLY TIME, a part of a bar at the tempo from 1/64 to 1/2, with triplets (T) and dotted values (D), and DLY BACK, how long they go on. REVERB at 0 takes the echoes out, so the low-pass has the kick and its repeats only. An LFO moves the rumble’s LEVEL (down to silence) and its DARK (up to 2 octaves each way); LFO PER BAR is the number of its cycles in one bar at the tempo, from 1/4 (one cycle in 4 bars) to 32, with 3, 5, 6 and 7. LFO PHASE is where in its cycle it starts, 0 to 357 degrees. The LFO wave button shows its wave in the picture, with a line where each cycle starts.
  12. 12. DuckDUCKThe body’s level envelope, upside down, lowers OP1 to OP3.

The trigger: 1 V, going up

  • An LFO’s full height is 5 V, as on the module your LFO text comes from. So 1 V is 20 % of the height, after the LFO’s LEVEL and OFFSET.
  • The kick plays when the LFO goes from below 20 % to 20 % or above. It can play again only after the LFO was below 20 %.
  • An LFO with RANGE at BOTH goes below zero: only its way up through +20 % plays the kick.
  • The bank moves 172 times a second, which is 5.8 ms between two moves. That is too coarse for a kick. So the engine finds where in those 5.8 ms the LFO started its step or crossed the level, and starts the kick on that sample.
  • All that an LFO has makes the rhythm: the ratio to the clock, the Euclidean pattern, PROB, the loop, the naps. A RATCHET gives rolls. An amount from another LFO on TRIGS changes the pattern as it plays.

The controls on the FM-1

OP6 becomes the kick’s key. A short press turns the kick on or off. Hold it 1 second to select it: the page is then the kick’s page, as for OP1 to OP3.

On this page K1 to K4 are KNOB1 to KNOB4, and ALGO is ALGORITHM.

The kick has three sets of knobs, as the LFO page has: the white keys 13, 14 and 15 choose BODY, CLICK or RUMBLE. The white keys 1 to 12 hit the kick by hand. The TEXTURE has no key and no knobs on the FM-1 yet: its controls on this page show NOT ON FM-1 YET where the others show their knob.

Choices and limits

The sound engine runs sample by sample.The browser plays the kick in its audio thread, at twice the sample rate, then filters it down, so the drive and the click make less false tone. The hits of the LFO land on the exact sample. A knob changes the sound while it plays.
The picture is the engine’s own output.The playhead shows where the sound is in the picture, from the audio clock. The picture is one hit from silence, so with the LFO on, the tail of the hit before is in the sound and not in the picture. The wave is one hit from silence, computed at 96 000 samples a second by the same code that makes the sound. Below one sample a pixel, each sample has its dot.
Three volumes, and KNOB1 is the volume in each set.The body’s LEVEL at 0 leaves the click and the rumble as they are. The click’s LEVEL also moves its noise. In FM and RING the click is inside the body: its LEVEL is then how much it bends or multiplies the body, and a silent body silences it.
The texture is beside the body, not in its place.The body has two outputs: the raw one, as before, and the texture. Each has its LEVEL. The rumble takes the raw body and the click, not the texture. The noise goes into the gate, so the gate shapes it with the body. The texture’s reverb is its own: it is brighter than the rumble’s. The texture’s knobs have no place on the FM-1 yet. A preset saved before the texture loads with the texture’s LEVEL at 0, so it sounds as it did.
The kick is not a style.It is a fourth voice with its own small engine, not a patch of blocks. It costs two sines, four envelopes, a noise and the rumble’s four echoes, less than a voice.
The wave and the curves are mine.The manual does not give them. I use a sine and curves that fall fast, then slowly, which is the common way to make this kind of kick. The ranges in the table above are a first estimate, to tune by ear on this page.
The drive is after the level envelope.The manual does not say where it is. After the envelope, the drive acts as a compressor on the start of the hit, which is what the manual describes.
The click is a second, short kick.It is the part that a kick module makes with the pitch envelope, taken out into an oscillator of its own. So the body can stay low and round (a small SWEEP), and you set the click where it cuts through: its note, its sweep, its attack, its length. In FM and RING it gives the body a hard start for the SNAP time.
MOD LEVEL is one slider for six knobs.TUNE, SNAP, SWEEP, ATTACK, PITCH CURVE and NOISE have a value at each marker. The markers are above the slider, 1 to 5 of them (with one marker the slider is off, and the knobs in the box are the click’s), and they share its travel in equal parts: with 3 markers, marker 2 is at the middle. Select a marker: MOD LEVEL goes to that marker, and the knobs in the box below show its values and change them. Between two markers the box shows the mix of the two markers, and the knobs do not move. Click above the slider between two markers: a new marker goes there with the mix of that place, and all the markers take equal places again. Remove Marker takes the selected marker out, after a confirmation. A preset keeps the 5 markers. A new kick has one marker, and so has a preset saved before MOD LEVEL. On the FM-1, an LFO of the bank can move MOD LEVEL; it has no knob there yet.
The body has a MOD LEVEL too.PITCH, DEPTH, SWEEP and PITCH CURVE have a value at each marker, with the same slider and the same markers as the click’s. The two MOD LEVELs are apart: one does not move the other. Between two markers the PITCH is between two notes. A preset keeps the 5 markers. A new kick has one marker, and so has a preset saved before the body’s MOD LEVEL. On the FM-1, an LFO of the bank can move it; it has no knob there yet.
The rumble’s attack is its own.Before, the rumble came up as the body went down. Now a long body can have a fast rumble, and a short body a slow one.
The rumble’s LFO on this page is a small one.Its rate is a number of cycles in a bar at the tempo, it has three shapes and a phase, and it starts its cycle when you start the LFO of the trigger. In the FM-1 it is an LFO of the bank, with all its shapes, steps and loops, through the amounts with the LFO key held. The page has the amounts on LEVEL and DARK only.
The delay and the reverb are the rumble’s own.They are not the DR1’s delay and reverb, and the body and the click do not go to the output through them. The delay needs memory in the firmware: half a bar at 40 BPM is 3 s, about 16 500 samples at one eighth of the sample rate.
The rumble is cheap.It is below 400 Hz, so the firmware can run it at one eighth of the sample rate: four echoes of about 300 samples each.
The rumble takes the kick, not the drones.The drones have the DR1’s delay and reverb. A send from the drones into the rumble is possible later.
DUCK lowers the three voices, not the delay and the reverb.The echoes and the tail stay up while the drones go down.
Ideas I did not put in.A drone voice as the kick’s FM source, so that the kick takes the colour of OP3. Velocity from the height of the LFO at the hit. A gate mode that holds the kick while the LFO is up.
No mixer strip in the first version.The kick has a level, sits in the centre and has no sends to the delay or the reverb. A fourth strip in the mixer is a second step.
OP4 and OP5 stay free.The same trigger can play other drums there later.
The processor’s load is not measured.The load of V0.33 with the new LFO bank is not known yet. Read it with SEL held before this goes in.
